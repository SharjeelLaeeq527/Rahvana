U.S. Immigrant Visa Process — NVC Through Post-Interview Outcomes

Internal developer reference  ·  CEAC case status mapping  ·  Process milestones & triggers
Developer Reference

This map covers the immigrant visa process from case arrival at the National Visa Center (NVC) through final post-interview outcomes. Each colored phase band groups related stages.

Three distinct state types are shown throughout:

  • Process milestone — An internal operational event or stage, not necessarily reflected in the public CEAC status checker.
  • CEAC document portal state — Labels visible inside the applicant's CEAC dashboard (e.g., Paid, Submitted, In Process). These are per-section statuses on the CEAC summary page, distinct from the public case status.
  • CEAC public case status — The status returned by the public CEAC Case Status tool when queried by case number. Marked with a black CEAC-visible tag wherever applicable.

The public CEAC case status typically remains At NVC across all internal NVC sub-stages — from fee payment through documentary qualification. It does not change with each internal step. This is a critical distinction for product developers.

Separate your data model layers. There are at least three distinct status spaces: (1) USCIS petition status, (2) CEAC document portal sub-statuses, and (3) the public CEAC case status. Do not conflate them.

Public CEAC status set for immigrant visa (IV) cases:

  • At NVC — Covers the entire NVC stage (fee payment through DQ).
  • In Transit — Case file transferred from NVC to embassy/consulate. Typically brief.
  • Ready — Embassy/consulate has received the case and is proceeding toward interview.
  • Administrative Processing — Post-interview additional review.
  • Issued — Visa approved. Foil being printed.
  • Refused — As of March 2020 DoS update, cases refused under INA §221(g) now show as Refused, not Administrative Processing. Covers both temporary/conditional and final denials.

Documentarily Qualified (DQ) is an internal NVC milestone, not a public CEAC status. Public status remains At NVC even after DQ.

Priority date dependency: For preference visa categories, NVC cannot schedule an interview unless the priority date is current per the monthly Visa Bulletin. Cases may remain At NVC in DQ state for months or years.

Primary sources consulted: U.S. Department of State travel.state.gov (NVC Processing, NVC Timeframes, CEAC Electronic Processing, CEAC FAQs, IV Scheduling Status Tool); DoS CEAC Case Status Change notice (March 2020, documents the §221(g) / Refused reclassification); cross-referenced against multiple immigration law firm reference guides.

Key verified facts: Public CEAC case status remains At NVC throughout all NVC internal sub-stages. As of March 3, 2020, cases refused under INA §221(g) show as Refused, not Administrative Processing. The distinction between a temporary 221(g) refusal and a final refusal is communicated via the written notice — not via CEAC status labels.

This artifact covers immediate relative, family preference, and employment preference cases. Does not cover diversity visas, special immigrant visas, K-1/K-3 fiancé(e) visas, or adoption cases.

8Post-Interview OutcomesThree possible CEAC-visible outcomes after adjudication — each carries distinct legal meaning
Visa Approved & Issued
The consular officer approves the immigrant visa. The visa foil is printed and placed in the applicant's passport. Applicant receives a sealed immigrant visa packet to present unopened to CBP upon U.S. entry.
Trigger
Officer approves application. All eligibility requirements met. No outstanding issues.
CEAC public case status tool shows
Issued
Final positive status. Visa foil in printing/passport return queue. Applicant must enter the U.S. before visa expiration (typically 6 months from medical exam date).
CEAC-visibleOutcome — Issued
Next: Applicant travels to U.S., presents sealed packet to CBP at port of entry. LPR status activated upon admission.
Administrative Processing
Case placed under additional review after interview. Reasons are generally not disclosed. May involve security checks, background verification, or other eligibility review. Distinct from a §221(g) refusal.
Trigger
Consular officer determines additional internal review is required. No action typically required from applicant unless the post requests specific documents.
CEAC public case status tool shows
Administrative Processing
As of March 2020 DoS update, cases refused under INA §221(g) now show as Refused, not Administrative Processing. AP now specifically reflects non-221(g) additional review.
CEAC-visibleOutcome — Pending
Timeline: No fixed duration. Typically weeks to months. CEAC status may not update during review. Embassy/consulate is the correct contact after 180 days.
Next: If cleared → Issued. If refused after review → Refused.
Refused
The consular officer enters a refusal. Covers two legally distinct situations that both display identically in CEAC. The written notice provided at interview distinguishes them.
INA §221(g) — Conditional / Temporary Refusal
Officer requires additional documents, further information, or pending advisory opinions before a final decision. Case remains active. Applicant has one year to respond. CEAC shows: Refused.
Final Denial — Grounds of Ineligibility
Officer finds applicant ineligible under a specific INA provision (e.g., §212 inadmissibility). Written notice specifies the grounds. CEAC shows: Refused.
CEAC public case status tool shows
Refused
CEAC does not distinguish 221(g) from final refusal. Distinction is communicated via the written notice only.
CEAC-visibleOutcome — Refused
221(g) path: Applicant provides requested documents → Post reviews → Status may update to Issued or remain Refused. Final denial: No further CEAC process; applicant may explore waivers (I-601) if applicable.
Next (221g): Submit requested documents to post per written instructions. Monitor CEAC for status update.
